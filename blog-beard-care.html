Beard

Beard care between cuts: how to soften and shape it

A tidy beard is mostly upkeep, not luck. Here's the short routine that keeps it soft and in line.
Structure Men's Studio · West Brickell, Miami

The line we set in the chair only lasts if you keep it up. Beard maintenance is quick — a wash, a balm, and a little discipline on the edges between visits.

Wash it (but not like your face)

Beard hair sits on skin that gets oily, so rinse it in the shower and wash it a couple of times a week. Facial skin is sensitive — a gentle hair shampoo is fine; harsh soap dries the skin underneath and leads to flaking.

Soften coarse hair with a balm

Coarse, wiry beards behave better with a little conditioning. A beard balm like Reuzel Beard Balm Original gives light control to soften the hair and shape it — so it lies the way you want instead of sticking out.

How to use beard balm

Scrape a small amount — less than you think — and rub it between your palms until it melts. Work it through the beard with the grain, then a little against it to reach the skin, and finish by combing or brushing down. Do it on a clean, towel-dried beard for the best hold.

Hold the line between visits

The neckline and cheek line are what make a beard look intentional. A rough rule: keep the neckline about two fingers above the Adam's apple and leave the cheek line where it naturally falls unless we've shaped it for you. When in doubt, leave it — it's easier to fix a beard that grew than one you over-trimmed.

Comb, don't just leave it

A quick comb each morning trains the hair in one direction and spreads the balm evenly. Over a few weeks that's what gives a beard a clean, deliberate shape rather than a bushy one.

Let us set the shape

Home upkeep keeps a beard tidy; the shape itself is worth setting in the chair, where we match it to your face and your haircut. Book a beard trim with your cut, and pick up a balm to hold it — see the collection on our grooming page.
